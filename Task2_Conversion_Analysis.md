# Task 2 — SaaS Conversion Analysis

**Question:** A SaaS platform providing Family Office Intelligence converts 3% of
free accounts to paying users. The founders want to increase MRR. How would you
improve the free-to-paid conversion rate?

## 1. Diagnose before prescribing

A 3% conversion rate is uninterpretable without context, so the first step is not a
fix — it's stating what I'd need to know: 3% of how many signups? Which personas are
in the funnel? Do users fail to activate, or activate and then decline to pay? What
is the price, and against what alternative? Naming the missing data is part of the
answer: I won't spend money changing a funnel I haven't diagnosed.

## 2. Lead hypothesis: wrong audience in the funnel

Family-office intelligence is a narrow, high-value B2B product, but a free tier
attracts students, researchers, and casual users with no budget or intent to buy.
They may find the product useful and still never convert. A low headline rate may
therefore reflect a **funnel-composition problem, not a product problem** — the free
tier is pulling in the wrong people.

## 3. First move — cheapest, highest information value

Before spending on any funnel change, segment existing *paying* users by acquisition
channel and persona. If payers cluster in one channel or one role, that single
finding reframes the entire problem and redirects spend before a dollar is wasted.
An operator sequences diagnostics by cost and information value, rather than running
everything at once.

## 4. Experiments, each tied to a hypothesis

- **Tighten free-tier qualification** at signup (role, firm type), so the funnel
  fills with plausible buyers rather than tire-kickers.
- **Prove value inside the trial:** ensure the user reaches a concrete "aha" — a
  real, actionable family-office lead they could not get elsewhere — *before* the
  trial expires.
- **Test packaging and price** against the institutional buyer (a family office /
  fund), not the individual, since B2B willingness-to-pay is set at the org level.

## 5. Metrics — and the trap in the question

Measured by activation rate, trial-to-paid conversion, CAC, and — critically —
**MRR itself.** There is a deliberate tension in the brief: tightening the funnel may
*lower* the headline 3% conversion rate while *raising* MRR, because fewer but
better-qualified users convert at higher value. The founders asked to increase
**MRR**, not the conversion percentage. I would optimize for their actual objective,
not the vanity metric — the two can point in opposite directions.
